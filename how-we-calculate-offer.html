The offer math

How America Home Buyers reviews an as-is property.

A direct offer is not a mystery number and it is not the same as a fully repaired retail price. We start with the likely resale value, then evaluate the real cost and risk of getting from the property’s current condition to a completed sale.

The honest tradeoff

Convenience has value, but so does market exposure.

If your house is market-ready and you have the time for showings, inspections, appraisal, and buyer financing, listing may produce a higher price. If the property needs substantial work or certainty matters more, a direct offer may be worth comparing.

The useful comparison is not simply offer price versus possible listing price. Compare what you may actually net after repairs, commissions or fees, concessions, carrying costs, and the time involved.

A good question to ask any buyer

“Please explain the repair assumptions and costs behind your offer.” A serious buyer should be able to discuss the major factors without hiding behind a slogan.

Do I need to repair the house before requesting an offer?

No. Seeing the property in its current condition helps us evaluate the real work involved.

Can the offer change after a walkthrough?

Material conditions that were unknown can affect an offer. Ask what due diligence remains and when the price becomes final before signing an agreement.

Should I get more than one opinion?

Yes. You may compare direct buyers, speak with a real estate agent about an as-is listing, and ask contractors about repair costs before deciding.
