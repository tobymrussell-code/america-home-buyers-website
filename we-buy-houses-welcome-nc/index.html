Start with the property as it is

You do not need a finished repair list before you call.

Some Welcome properties are straightforward houses. Others include older systems, acreage, garages, sheds, personal property, or years of deferred maintenance. Those details should be discussed early instead of treated like surprises later.

  • Repair-heavy or dated houses.
  • Inherited properties with belongings still inside.
  • Vacant homes and delayed maintenance.
  • Land, garages, sheds, or cleanup work.

Know the tradeoff

Compare the direct offer with the likely listing net.

A market-ready property may do better through a traditional listing. A direct sale may fit when the repair work, cleanout, showings, timeline, or uncertainty are the bigger concern. The useful number is what remains after the work and selling costs—not just the headline price.

Can I sell without making repairs?

Yes. We can review the property in its current condition before you decide whether repairing or listing is worthwhile.

Can land, garages, or outbuildings be included?

Yes. Describe the entire property so acreage, access, structures, and belongings can be included in the review.

Is requesting an offer binding?

No. A property review does not obligate you to sell. Any sale requires mutually accepted written terms.

Include acreage, outbuildings and access in the review

For a Welcome property, explain whether the sale includes land, storage buildings, a garage or equipment that needs to remain. A house-only estimate may miss the cleanup or access work involved. If the property uses a well or septic system, share what you know about its condition and available records.

For land without a recognized street address, use the parcel or road description and nearest town in the form. An address-lookup match is helpful, but it is not required to start the conversation.
