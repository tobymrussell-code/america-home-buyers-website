How we bought an inherited house as-is on Unity Street

The owners lived about four hours away in Myrtle Beach. With years of unpaid property taxes and an auction ahead, they sold the house as-is and signed from home.

By Toby Russell, founder of America Home Buyers LLC

They sold their inherited house without making the four-hour drive

I mailed them a postcard about the Unity Street house, and they called me from Myrtle Beach. They’d inherited the house and didn’t want to keep it. The property taxes were five or six years behind, and they were getting notices that the house was headed for a tax foreclosure auction.

While the house sat vacant, people got inside, pulled wiring out of the walls, and left a mess. The owners wanted to sell without driving back to Thomasville to make repairs or clean it out.

Once we agreed on a price they were happy with, I drove to Myrtle Beach and paid for a notary to come to their home. They signed the offer and closing paperwork from home. We bought the house as-is, and they got paid without making repairs, cleaning it out, or making the trip back to North Carolina.

What happened after we bought it

Clearing the rooms was the first part. The property went through a full renovation, including heating and cooling work, and ultimately became a four-bedroom, two-bath rental in Thomasville.

If you inherited a house with belongings inside

Start by telling me what the family wants to keep, what would stay, and who can let me into the house. Tell me about repairs you already know about. We can look at the house as it sits and talk through how the cleanout affects the offer.

If you’re weighing a direct offer against listing, compare what you would keep after cleanout, repairs, and sale expenses. Our guide to selling an inherited house in North Carolina walks through what to sort out before you decide. You can also read what to put in writing when belongings stay with the house.
